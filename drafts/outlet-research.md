# Outlet research — where to pitch The Closing Window (2026-08)

Compiled via web research 2026-08-09. Verify flagged items before
sending; pitch pages change.

**Ranking rationale:** fit-times-feasibility — outlets that publish
exactly this format (data-driven visual/argument essays) with
verified, currently-open freelance channels first; prestige outlets
with vaguer intake middle; quick-win and niche/trade last.

## Tier 1 — Best fit, verified open channels

### 1. The Pudding
- **Why:** The single best fit — original-dataset visual essays about
  culture with a thesis. A 20-year, 3,130-designer dataset with a
  dated structural break is a textbook Pudding pitch.
- **Format:** Custom visual essay (multi-month; they accommodate
  contributors with day jobs).
- **Pitch:** form at pudding.cool/pitch or pitches@pudding.cool. Open
  year-round; reviewed monthly at the start of each month, feedback
  within a week either way. Include rough data methodology; full
  analysis not required. Wants a debatable thesis + why the story is
  uniquely visual. Explicit no-AI-generated-pitches policy.
- **Pay:** $7,200 standard for end-to-end freelance (reporting,
  analysis, design, dev); scales down if their team handles
  production. Being an engineer who can build the interactive
  maximizes fit and fee.

### 2. Asterisk Magazine
- **Why:** Data-literate essays on culture/economics/institutions;
  "what mechanically changed in 2015" is their kind of question.
  Actively solicits non-journalist subject-matter experts.
- **Format:** Essay up to ~4,000 words, quarterly themed issues.
- **Pitch:** 2–3 paragraphs + writing sample to
  submissions@asteriskmag.com; guide at asteriskmag.com/pitch-guide.
  Currently soliciting for Issue 16 "Flourishing" (Fall 2026) — the
  can-you-still-make-a-living-from-craft angle could fit the theme.
- **Pay:** $2,000/story (public).

### 3. Works in Progress
- **Why:** Data-heavy argument essays; loves "a dataset that
  overturns a folk narrative." The no-algorithm-marketplace-still-
  produced-superstars angle fits their economics sensibility.
- **Format:** 2–5k words, chart-heavy; shorter "Notes on Progress"
  section possible.
- **Pitch:** 1–3 short paragraphs (claim + core facts) to
  wip-pitches@stripe.com. Process: pitch → 1–2 page outline → draft.
  Explicit no-AI-written-pitches policy.
- **Pay:** not published; negotiated.

### 4. Aeon
- **Why:** Longform ideas essays for a general intelligent reader;
  welcomes non-academics with professional expertise.
- **Format:** 2,500–5,000 words, 3–5 drafts, professional fact-check.
- **Pitch:** aeon.co/pitch — portal reportedly open only the FIRST
  WEEK of each month (VERIFY on the page; detail from secondary
  sources).
- **Pay:** pays all contributors; rate not published.

### 5. Slate (Business & Tech, or Internet Culture)
- **Why:** Data-informed features on platforms reshaping work and
  culture; "one generous year minted today's stars" is very Slate.
  Named editors on the official pitch page (slate.com/pitch):
  - Business & Tech: Tony Ho Tran — tony.tran@slate.com ($500–800
    cited in 2026 calls)
  - Internet culture/essays: Jenny G. Zhang — jenny.zhang@slate.com
  - Longform 5,000+: Jeffrey Bloomer — jeffrey.bloomer@slate.com /
    Leah Prinzivalli — leah.prinzivalli@slate.com
- **Format:** 1,200–2,000 word feature; longform desk for 5,000+.
- **Pay:** ~$350 baseline culture; $500–800 business/tech.

## Tier 2 — Strong fits, higher bar or narrower angle

### 6. Noema — platform-society essays with philosophical ambition.
  Pitch via noemamag.com/contact ("Pitch Us"); prefers full drafts or
  500+ word pitches. Reported ~$1/word, ~$3,000 essays (confirm).
### 7. Longreads — researched essays 2–6k words; the ONLY outlet here
  explicitly allowing simultaneous submissions. hello@longreads.com,
  subject line = pitch type + topic. Strict no-generative-AI policy;
  payment net-60; no response unless interested. From $500 (essays) /
  $0.50/word (reported).
### 8. WIRED — Ideas op-ed (complete draft <1,000 words, loses the
  charts) via opinion@wired.com "Op-Ed Pitch" + thesis + bio; or
  pitch a feature editor directly (firstname_lastname@wired.com).
  Up to ~$2,500 features; op-eds often unpaid. FLAG: guidance partly
  traces to a pre-2023 pitch guide — verify channel.
### 9. Vox — explainer-native. Route via
  vox.com/pages/how-to-pitch-vox-pitching-guidelines;
  firstperson@vox.com for personal essays (~$500). $0.50–1+/word.
  FLAG: culture-desk editor emails not verifiable from current
  public sources.
### 10. The Atlantic — half freelance; current advice from a senior
  editor (July 2026): new writers should start with a timely-but-not-
  time-sensitive 800–1,500 word piece, not a 3,000-word feature.
  Widely cited: ideas@theatlantic.com, submissions@theatlantic.com.
  FLAG: emails from secondary sources only — double-check.

## Tier 3 — Quick wins, niche, and trade

### 11. Dirt (dirt.fyi) — 500–1,000 word sharp version ("Ravelry never
  had an algorithm and its creator economy broke anyway"); fast
  byline-builder. editors@dirt.fyi; $0.50/word standard. FLAG: skim
  dirt.fyi/dirt-pitch-guidelines before sending.
### 12. The Walrus — features/visual essays, but prioritizes Canadian
  relevance; would need a Canadian angle. thewalrus.ca/about/
  submissions. $500 (~1,000 words) up to $1/word.
### 13. Nightingale (Data Visualization Society) — ideal for a
  METHODS companion piece (how the 2015 break was dated), which also
  markets the main essay. Requires free DVS membership;
  nightingaledvs.com/article-submission. Assume low/no pay; value is
  audience.
### 14. Craft Industry Alliance — the knitting industry's trade pub;
  West/Mowry/PetiteKnit material lands with the people it's about.
  hello@craftindustryalliance.org; pays, rate not public. Smaller
  reach, likely the fastest yes.
### 15. The Baffler — polemical cut ("the myth of the meritocratic
  marketplace"), 2,200–5,000 words; PITCH ONLY (two paragraphs), no
  drafts, via thebaffler.com/about/submissions. Pay reports conflict
  ($0.13 vs $0.50/word) — confirm.

## Not currently viable

- **Hazlitt:** submissions currently CLOSED (verified). Watch for
  reopening.
- **Embedded:** no freelance pipeline; better play is their
  "My Internet" Q&A featuring becca + the dataset as promo.
- **The Verge:** no current public pitch guidelines; only a stale
  2019 paywalled guide. Cold-email a current culture editor if at
  all; low priority.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Simultaneous submissions:** only Longreads explicitly permits.
  Stagger the big pitches — Pudding first (monthly review, ~1 week
  feedback after the review meeting).
- **AI policies:** The Pudding, Works in Progress, and Longreads have
  explicit no-AI-written-pitch policies. The cold pitch being built
  from becca's own email prose matters here — final wording should be
  hers throughout.
- **Suggested sequencing:** (1) The Pudding + Nightingale methods
  companion; (2) if Pudding passes, split the material:
  Asterisk/WiP/Aeon for the argument essay, Slate/Dirt for the
  news-hook version, Craft Industry Alliance for the trade version.
  The dataset supports multiple non-competing pieces.
- **Verify-before-sending:** Aeon's first-week window; Atlantic
  emails; WIRED opinion@ channel; Dirt guidelines page; Baffler pay.

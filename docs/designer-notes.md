## Andrea Mowry

Andrea Mowry has always been a bit of an enigma. This isn't the first time I have been curious about who she is and how she started, but I have never found a bio. She doesn't even say where she lives. She is constantly taking photos of herself, perfectly styled in her colorful knits, but there's not much to find underneath that image.

Here's what I can piece together from the Instagram record. In 2014, she was just a mom posting photos of her pregnancies and her kids and occasional knitting projects. She had a very typical millennial #vscocam aesthetic.

Her project photos in this era were largely flat-lay, not photos with her modeling them. Soon (by mid 2014) she started modeling her completed sweaters (not her own designs yet), and picking up some regular visitors and commenters just with her photos.

In August 2014, one of her finished projects was featured in a Ravelry thread. SSKALONG. She started doing more knit-a-long content, photos started looking more curated and very 2014-instagram-era aesthetic. intentional flat-lays, etc.

During this time, she was also posting about vegan recipes, but her knitting posts consistently got more likes.

In October 2014, she posted a photo of a KAL project that was liked by stephen west. Her average like counts started increasing after this, even for non-knitting content. She always tagged the project, the designers, and the yarn companies. She tended to gravitate towards high-end yarn like brooklyn tweed and spincycle, even in those days.

So, the story I am slowly piecing together is that she became known on Ravelry before she ever published her own patterns. She was prolific on instagram and participated in KALs, so people started paying attention to her work before she started designing just because she was a skilled marketer.

The instagram records shows that one of her first patterns on Ravelry (baby mittens) was published in 2014. Is our record from ravelry different?

So... it has taken me a long time just to scroll through late 2014. She was prolific in her posting and her knitting during this time. Her designing and KAL networking were beginning around the same time.

Her husband is her photographer, that's an interesting data point.

By the end of 2014, it's pretty clear she's trying to turn this into a business. She was consistently getting > 100 likes on her knitting posts, including from some well-known designers and brand accounts. She started designing in 2014, and in December she was selling her first pattern collection. (How did she have time do all of this? She also had kids!)

Very professional-coded language during this time, sales, gift codes, etc. In this era her posts were getting 100-200 likes on average, so she was getting some traction, but not celebrity-level yet. But it's clear the business was intentional. She was discovered on Ravelry, but within a couple of months she was turning it into a full-blown online business. There's clearly a drive there to build her brand.

The networking is visible. She is promoting knitting podcasters, tagging designers, using hashtags to increase her visibility, collaborating with other creators, getting test knitters. So here's the pattern I'm observing - yes her discovery on Ravelry came first, she was tagged in a KAL thread and more people started paying attention to her project posts. But then she IMMEDIATELY started hustling. By the end of the year she was publishing pattern collections, BEFORE she was a recognizable name. It's like she knew there was a window in 2014, and tried to charge through it.

In January 2015, she refers to her knitting as a business that she is building while she is staying home with her baby and cooking vegan meals every afternoon. She is also completing several projects and publishing multiple patterns every month. (Again, how does she have time for this? I respect the hustle, but does she have any friends? Other hobbies? Has she spent any time with her husband?)

In February 2015, she documents her first knitting event, StitchesWest. She is consistently getting 200-300 likes on he posts in early 2015. She shows herself teaching in June.

We should definitely document this pattern release cadence and compare it to others in different cohorts.

Just a personal observation that I won't include in my writing - she was such an Instagram girl in that era that it almost seems cringe now. The constant posting, the perfect aesthetic, the vegan cooking, the quirky tattoos and intentional styling, it seems unattainable in a way that didn't age very well. It's curated and intentional and hashtagged and VSCO-filtered in a way that comes off pretty inauthentic now.

She was having Instagram sales on her profile probably before she had very many customers. It's not just that she's using instagram to build her business, Instagram IS her business just as much as the pattern design.

I recognize that some of this is probably my own bias. Women who really hustle for their notoriety often come off as less authentic than the people who were just "discovered". Visible ambition can be a turn-off, even though the ambition, the constant posting, the instagram aesthetic, the networking, the immediate move from KALs to paid patterns with giveaways and discount codes, is probably what it took to succeed and build the career she wanted in that era.

She photographs herself with Stephen West in August 2015 with the hashtag #wannabemybestiewestie

In December 2015 she starts doing Periscope live streams. Her posts are getting between 600 and 800 likes on average. (just manually scanning, no calculator was involved here)

In the beginning of 2016, she is visibly pregnant. On May 5, 2016, she photographs herself knitting while at the hospital in labor. (and gets 2000 likes).

On May 6th 2016, she posts a photo of her new baby.

On May 7th 2016, she posts about a hedgehog fibers giveaway that she is running on her blog.

On May 15th, she's posting on her blog about swatching. A few days later, she's looking for test knitters for her latest shawl pattern (the one that she was knitting in the hostpital).

It's interesting that there is a thing that almost never gets mentioned: child care. Every single mother of a baby or toddler knows that despite the number of photos she takes with a cup of coffee, knitting needles, and a baby peacefully sleeping next to her, that's just not reality. The only way that women have time to build a business AND take care of their baby is by having child care. It was believable that Lucy from Attic24 was just stitching during nap time, because she wasn't hustling in the same way. But it's really obvious to me that Andrea wasn't releasing two patterns a month, running knit-a-longs, traveling to knitting events, and curating daily social media posts while also being a full-time parent. So even her story seems a bit... inauthentic. Motherhood doesn't free women to find their passion unless someone else is doing part of the mothering.
